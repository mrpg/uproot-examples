Cards and list groups put related content into a visible box. Cards suit self-contained units, such as the options of a choice, the parts of a study, or a partner’s profile. List groups suit sequences of short items, such as steps, earnings, or a checklist.

Anatomy of a card

A card is a .card with up to three parts: an optional .card-header, a .card-body, and an optional .card-footer. Inside the body, .card-title, .card-subtitle, and .card-text fix the spacing of the typical elements. For a card without header and footer, uproot’s examples often use the shortcut class="card card-body".

Key classes: .card, .card-header, .card-body, .card-title, .card-subtitle, .card-text, .card-footer

Colors and borders

Cards take border and background utilities. .text-bg-* sets a background together with a readable text color. The subtle variants (.bg-*-subtle with .border-*-subtle) are calmer and good for status. A transparent header (.bg-transparent) keeps a colored border card light.

Key classes: .border-success, .text-bg-primary, .bg-secondary-subtle, .border-secondary-subtle, .bg-transparent

Grids of cards with equal heights

To show options side by side, put cards into a grid: .row-cols-1 .row-cols-md-3 stacks them on phones and shows three per row from tablets on. Cards in a row only have equal heights with .h-100. .card-footer then sits at the bottom of each card, even if the bodies have different lengths.

Key classes: .row-cols-*, .g-*, .h-100

List groups

A list group is a bordered list. Items can carry a badge on the right (with .d-flex .justify-content-between), be numbered (.list-group-numbered on an <ol>), be colored, or be marked as active or disabled. .list-group-flush removes the outer borders, so that a list fits seamlessly into a card.

Key classes: .list-group, .list-group-item, .list-group-numbered, .list-group-flush, .list-group-item-success (and other colors), .active, .disabled, .list-group-horizontal-md
