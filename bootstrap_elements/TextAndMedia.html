Most experiment pages are mostly text: consent forms, instructions, and comprehension questions. Bootstrap’s defaults are already readable, so this chapter is about the tools that add structure and emphasis. For uproot’s fonts and font weights, see the templating_and_typography app.

Headings and display text

Use <h2> to <h6> for the structure of a page (the page title is already an <h1>). If a heading should look larger or smaller than its level, keep the correct element and add a class such as .h4: screen readers use the element, not its looks. Display classes make single numbers or words stand out, for example a participant’s payoff.

Key classes: .h1 to .h6, .display-1 to .display-6, .fs-1 to .fs-6

Lead paragraphs and secondary text

A lead paragraph introduces a page (every chapter of this walkthrough starts with one). For less important text, use <small> or .small and a muted color.

Key classes: .lead, .small, .text-body-secondary, .text-body-tertiary

In this part of the study, you will make ten decisions about how to split money.

Each decision is independent. At the end, one decision is randomly selected for payment.

All amounts are in US dollars. Your decisions remain anonymous.

Inline text elements

HTML has elements for many kinds of emphasis, and Bootstrap styles them consistently. <strong> and <em> carry meaning (importance and stress), whereas .fw-bold and .fst-italic only change the looks.

  • Important: you cannot change your decision later. (<strong>)
  • The other participant does not know your identity. (<em>)
  • Read the highlighted part carefully. (<mark>)
  • Press Space as soon as the circle turns green. (<kbd>)
  • The MPCR is 0.4. (<abbr>; hover over it)
  • Price: $10.00 $8.00 (<del> and <ins>)

Text utilities

Utility classes change alignment, wrapping, case, weight, and line height. Like most classes, alignment takes breakpoints: .text-center .text-md-start is centered on phones and left-aligned from tablets on.

Key classes: .text-start, .text-center, .text-end, .text-nowrap, .text-truncate, .text-break, .text-uppercase, .fw-*, .fst-italic, .lh-*, .font-monospace

Links

Bootstrap 5.3 has utilities for link colors, underline offset, and underline opacity. .icon-link aligns an icon with the link text. Use target="_blank" for links that participants should open without leaving the study.

Key classes: .link-primary (and other colors), .link-offset-2, .link-underline-opacity-25, .link-underline-opacity-100-hover, .icon-link, .link-subtle (uproot)

Lists

Numbered lists are the natural format for steps, bullet lists for unordered facts. .list-unstyled removes bullets (useful with icons, as on the welcome page), and .list-inline puts items on one line. A description list in the grid is a compact way to summarize parameters.

Key classes: .list-unstyled, .list-inline, .list-inline-item; <dl class="row"> with .col-* on <dt> and <dd>

Quotes and vignettes

Vignette studies present a scenario that participants respond to. A blockquote sets it apart from the instructions around it. Wrap it in a <figure> to add a source.

Key classes: .blockquote, .blockquote-footer, .callout (uproot)

The same scenario as a callout: an .alert with uproot’s .callout class, which keeps only a thick left border.

Images and figures

Always add .img-fluid to images: it scales them down on small screens instead of letting them overflow. Use <figure> with a caption for stimuli or charts. Give every image an alt text that describes it, or alt="" if it is purely decorative. Serve your own images from your app’s _static folder with appstatic() (see the embed_static app); the example uses uproot’s logo.

Key classes: .img-fluid, .img-thumbnail, .rounded, .rounded-circle, .figure, .figure-img, .figure-caption, .object-fit-cover
